Discovery Brief: Metz/Chef2Home Health Cloud 2026

Status: Discovery, 2026-11-09 · Sources: client scoping questionnaire v2, client service codes and modifiers sheet, chef2home.com, public web research · Products in scope: Health Cloud (Agentforce Health), MuleSoft

Confidence labels: Confirmed = the client stated it in writing; Assumed = a working assumption the Solution Lead set or endorsed, pending client confirmation; Unknown = not yet answered.

Executive Summary

Chef2Home by Metz delivers medically tailored home meals paid for by Ohio Medicaid waivers, MyCare managed care plans, Area Agencies on Aging (AAAs), the Ohio Department of Developmental Disabilities (DODD) and county levies. It wants Health Cloud added to its existing Chef2Home Salesforce org for the whole referral-to-cash cycle: - Referral intake: document automation for about 1,400 referrals a week. - Funding sources: one Care Program per funding source. - Claims: 837P claims sent out through MuleSoft to seven clearinghouses, and 835 remittance files read back in to drive claim status and rebilling.

The client's target is go-live on 29 January 2027, with billing live that same day. That date is the top risk, because seven clearinghouse connections must be set up and tested with outside parties in the same window.

Company and Industry Context

  • Who they are. Chef2Home is a healthcare meal service line of Metz Culinary Management, LLC (Dallas, PA; founded 1994; CEO Jeff Metz). Metz has served senior meals since 1996 and launched Chef2Home in June 2022, producing meals at a USDA-approved culinary center in Sarasota, FL. (Confirmed: chef2home.com, metzculinary.com)
  • The Ohio operation. Global Meals (Columbus, OH) became "a division of Chef2Home by Metz" in June 2024 and serves all 88 Ohio counties (and Indiana). Its published funders are PASSPORT, the Ohio Home Care Waiver, DODD, the Franklin County Senior Options levy, McGregor PACE (Program of All-Inclusive Care for the Elderly) and AAAs for planning areas 1–11. These match the client's service-code sheet. (Confirmed public source. That this is the operation in scope is inferred.)
  • Scope boundary. Ohio only for this phase. Every payer on the service-code sheet is in Ohio. (Assumed: Solution Lead, 2026-11-09)
  • Who pays and who refers. A case manager at a payer or agency refers the client and is linked to the client's Funding Source. There are about 40 funding sources. A client has only one active funding source at a time; moving between payers is a Funding Source change. (Confirmed)

Data and Compliance Considerations

In the client's words, "I don't think there is anything difficult… the ability to lock records down to not be editable at a certain point is the main requirement." The lock trigger points are still open.
  • History. Funding-source, delivery and claim history must survive payer changes, denials, rebills and authorization updates. Field history tracking and record locks apply.
  • Protected health information. The client hasn't stated HIPAA, Shield or retention requirements.
  • Migration. The old system's name and record volumes are Unknown. The scope above assumes about 13 months of history.
  • Person accounts. Turning them on is permanent. Clients are Contacts today; converting them to person accounts is in scope (ADR 0002, amended 2026-11-09).

Research Findings and Market Context

  • Ohio billing context. Ohio Home Care Waiver home-delivered meals bill per meal under S5170 (Ohio Administrative Code 5160-46-06, effective 17 April 2026). The client's service-code sheet confirms S5170 with payer-specific modifiers across the waiver, MyCare and PASSPORT.
  • Managed care churn. Next Generation MyCare Ohio went live on 1 January 2026 with four plans, including Anthem and CareSource, and is expanding statewide through 2026. Clients moving between plans is expected to continue, which is why ADR 0003 is designed around Funding Source changes.
  • Policy headwind. In March 2025 the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) rescinded its health-related social needs (HRSN) waiver framework. Existing approvals stand. This adds uncertainty for new food-as-medicine funding, not for current waiver billing.
  • Competitors. Mom's Meals (PurFoods), GA Foods/SunMeadow, Tangelo and Homestyle Direct. Mom's Meals runs a national case-manager referral portal; portal intake is the market direction, consistent with the client's expected tripling of portal referrals.
  • Not found. No public evidence of the client's Salesforce or Certinia use. Revenue and headcount figures are third-party estimates only and are left out here.

Solution: Metz/Chef2Home Health Cloud 2026

Status: Design, 2026-11-09 · Products in scope: Health Cloud (Agentforce Health), MuleSoft · Builds on: outputs/00-discovery-brief.md, ADRs 0001–0003, 10 epics, 136 gaps

Source tags: [KA-XXXX] = central Salesforce knowledge base article; [docs: …] = Salesforce/MuleSoft product documentation; [extends: …] = a grounded pattern applied to a slightly different use; [assumption: …] = general implementation judgment, with what would validate it. 🔴 marks a decision still open.

Summary

Chef2Home gets one referral-to-cash flow inside its existing Salesforce org: - Referral documents arrive by email, fax and portal and become Health Cloud referrals. - Each client is enrolled in the Care Program for their funding source, with a coverage period that holds the authorization. - Every delivery becomes a claim line. - MuleSoft turns submitted batches into 837P files for seven clearinghouses and reads 835 remittance files back. - Each payment result is matched to the exact delivery and kept as history.

Supporting this: - A permission model sized for protected health information. - Record locks for the annual audit. - A rehearsed Contact-to-person-account conversion. - A shared release pipeline, because Metz's own team keeps building scheduling in the same org.

Architecture Foundations

Org strategy

  • One org. Health Cloud is built in the existing Chef2Home production org, through the new full sandbox. The separate Metz org that runs Certinia is untouched (ADR 0001, client-supplied).
  • Service Cloud. Live Service Cloud gets a regression pass before each production deploy.
  • Person accounts. Health Cloud requires person accounts for clients [KA-41084].
  • Turning them on can't be undone [KA-41826].
  • Clients are Contacts today, and converting them is in scope (ADR 0002, amended).
  • Person accounts are turned on in production as a planned cutover step, after the conversion rehearsal passes in the full sandbox (see Data Migration below).

Sharing model and protected health information

  • Defaults. Org-wide defaults are Private for person accounts, care program enrollments, coverage periods, claims and remittance records.
  • Access by role. Access comes from one permission set group per persona [KA-10394]:
Persona Access
Intake specialists (7) referrals, documents, client create
Service reps client read, referral read
Billing claims, remittance, coverage periods
Route supervisors delivery exceptions, missed-delivery alerts
Drivers existing mobile app access, unchanged
  • Field-level security. Clinical and diagnosis fields extracted from referrals are restricted by field-level security.
  • Shield and retention are still open (G0015, G0901). Standard field history is kept for 18 months in the UI and 24 months through the API [KA-11166][KA-11519]. Keeping it longer means a scheduled export or Field Audit Trail [KA-11335][KA-11433]. The account executive owns licensing.

Environments and release management

  • Starting point. Today, Metz's internal team changes delivery and scheduling objects in the same production org. Discovery named no source control or release process (G0105).
  • Pipeline. One source-controlled pipeline is shared by both teams (Salesforce PS and Metz) [KA-27285].
  • Tooling is either DevOps Center/Lifecycle Center or a Salesforce CLI pipeline. Metz chooses and owns it.
  • Change sets and DX Inspector don't fit two teams releasing into one org [KA-27347].
  • With more than one team in the org, source-driven development is the standard recommendation.
  • Environments.
Environment Use
Developer sandboxes Build
The new full sandbox Integration testing, the conversion rehearsal and UAT
Production Live org
  • Refreshes. Refreshing a sandbox breaks its pipeline stage. Refreshes follow an agreed calendar, and the refreshed environment is reconnected to the pipeline afterwards [KA-27219]. Protected health information in lower environments is masked [KA-28108][KA-44327].
  • Release calendar. One calendar for both teams, with code freezes around the person-account switch-on and the go-live deploy.

Governance and run state

  • Who runs it. Metz runs the system after go-live.
  • Billing setup is data, not code. Code mappings, payer rebill rules and clearinghouse routing live in records the billing team edits. They don't need a release.
  • Change management. An optional change management consultant covers intake and billing adoption. If Metz declines it, Metz owns enablement.

Solution by Business Process

Ordered by how value flows: client record → referral → funding → delivery → claim → submission → payment → audit, then migration and program.

1. Health Cloud Foundation and Org Readiness (E01)

Business context: Clients are Contacts in a Service Cloud org today. Caregivers and advocates aren't modeled, and nothing separates protected health information from general service data.

Solution approach: - Install Health Cloud in the existing org, with clients as person accounts. - Model family caregivers, spouses and advocates in the care network [KA-2459][KA-41041]. - Set up the permission set groups described under Foundations.

Supporting architecture: - Care network relationship types and any signing or speaking rights are open (G0017). - Service Cloud regression covers the existing keyword email flow, Cases and the drivers' mobile app.

2. Referral Intake and Document Automation (E02)

Business context: About 1,400 referrals a week reach 7 intake specialists: 71% by email, 22% by eFax and 7% through a partner portal, with portal volume expected to triple. An email flow sets High/Med/Low priority by keyword. Specialists key in everything else.

Solution approach: Every channel lands as a Health Cloud Received Document. - Intelligent Document Reader pulls out the case manager, client, address changes and priority keywords. - The Process Received Document action creates the referral (Clinical Service Request) [KA-41819]. - Specialists review it in priority triage queues.

Supporting architecture: - Email (resolves G0201). - Metz IT sets up the Office 365 referral mailbox to decrypt on the mail server, so messages arrive as standard emails with PDF attachments [assumption: Metz IT can configure server-side decryption; client responsibility, validate with Metz IT]. - MuleSoft reads the mailbox, splits emails that carry several attachments, and makes one Composite API call per PDF. That call creates the Content Document and the Received Document and links them (PDF, JPG or PNG, under 35 MB) [KA-41472]. - A body-only email goes to the review queue as-is. - Fax. Fax uses Health Cloud's native fax intake [KA-41290]. - Portal. Portal uploads create the same Received Document record. - Extraction. Health Cloud's Intelligent Document Reader with Amazon Textract does the extraction [KA-41019]. Using it rather than a separate MuleSoft parser keeps one extraction engine for all channels. The account executive owns the add-on licensing. - Routing. - Two queues: one for newly received documents and one for processed documents. - A record-triggered flow moves each document between them. The flow that fires when text extraction succeeds runs asynchronously [KA-41473][KA-41819]. - Extracted keywords drive the existing High/Med/Low triage into priority queues [extends: KA-41819 flow routing, with keyword logic on the extracted text]. - Address changes. An extracted address change is staged on the referral for a specialist to confirm. It never overwrites the client's address automatically. - Matching. Matching the referral to existing case managers and clients is an extra on top of the core flow.

3. Funding Sources, Care Programs and Authorizations (E03)

Business context: - About 40 funding sources pay for meals. - A client has one active funding source at a time and moves between payers often, especially under Next Generation MyCare. - Authorizations arrive on the referral documents. - The modifier billed depends on both the payer and the meal type.

Solution approach: - Each funding source is a Care Program. - Each client has one enrollment per program, reopened when the client returns [KA-18914]. - A coverage-period child record holds the dates, the authorization reference and the active flag, and only one coverage period can be active across all of a client's enrollments (ADR 0003). Authorizations are captured onto the coverage period [KA-41246][KA-41971]. - A Funding Source change closes one coverage period and opens another.

Supporting architecture: - Billing code mapping is a data object the billing team maintains. It's keyed on funding source × meal type and returns the procedure code, modifier and units rule. Examples: S5170 with U6, U7, UA or UA:U7, and DODD's AMN/FMN/SMN codes. - Custom metadata is rejected because every change would need a deployment [KA-38798][KA-0026]. - [assumption: meal type is stored per client or per authorization; validate with G0301] - Units rules are settled in detailed design (G0003). - Payer rebill style is a setting on the same billing setup (see Claims). - The existing Funding Source object (G0305) is decided in detailed design, after reviewing the object and everything that references it. - We lean toward reusing it as the coverage-period child under the enrollment if Metz's scheduling objects depend on it, which saves re-pointing their lookups and flows. - Existing rows with repeat stints collapse into one enrollment with several coverage periods [KA-18914]. "Multiple Enrollments in the Same Care Program" stays off. - Change history. Field history is tracked on enrollments and coverage periods.

4. Missed-Delivery Alert (E07)

Business context: Drivers already use the Salesforce mobile app. Route supervisors hear about a missed delivery too late to recover the route.

Solution approach: - When a driver flags a missed delivery, a record-triggered flow sends a Salesforce custom notification to the route supervisors, on desktop and mobile push [KA-35168][KA-39156][KA-39159]. - Slack is not used.

Supporting architecture: - Message limits. Notification text is plain and character-limited [KA-39155]. - Lock screen. The "Display full content push notifications" setting stays off, so no protected health information shows on a lock screen. The notification links to the record instead. - Delivery objects are Metz's. The missed-delivery flag belongs to Metz's own delivery objects. We add only the trigger and the notification. - Open. The exact trigger and whether supervisors also need a console list view are open (G0013).

5. Claims and Billing Operations (E04)

Business context: - Today a biller clicks a button in the old system to create a batch, then reviews claims by status by hand. - There's no denial automation. - The old system's renewal ends 31 March 2027.

Solution approach: - Claims and claim lines live in Health Cloud, with one claim line per delivery, each carrying its own line ID [KA-0004][KA-13626]. - A biller starts a batch for one payer, reviews it, then submits it. - Billing list views by status, and a mass Denied/Partial Pay → Rebill update [KA-39149][KA-35220]. - Submitted claims lock (see Audit).

Supporting architecture: - Status lifecycle. Ready → Batched → Submitted → Paid / Denied / Partial Pay → Rebill. - Object choice. Standard Insurance Claim/Claim Item [KA-18465][KA-17487] vs custom claim objects is decided in detailed design. 🟡 The knowledge base's Health Cloud claims material is payer-side [KA-41695]. - Line shape. The line ID, not the delivery ID alone, is the matching key. That's because Metz may deliver more than once a day, and a per-day payer may split one delivery into several lines (G0401, G0003). - Rebill (resolves G0404). - The payer's claim control number is captured from every 835 and stored on the remittance record. That supports both rebill styles: a new original claim, or a replacement (frequency code 7) or void (code 8) that quotes the payer's claim number. - Each payer's style is configured in the billing setup [assumption: confirm per-payer rules from Metz's saved 837 rebills and companion guides (G0002)]. - Moving a delivery to a different payer after a denial is a new claim to that payer, not a rebill (G0304).

6. 837P Claim Submission Integration (E05)

Business context: Claims go to seven clearinghouses (Availity, Office Ally and others), and each payer has its own companion guide.

Solution approach: - Submitting a batch publishes an event that MuleSoft subscribes to [KA-0141][KA-0445]. - MuleSoft pulls the claim lines, applies the billing code mapping and builds a HIPAA 837P file. - It sends the file automatically to the right clearinghouse, over SFTP or API.

Supporting architecture: - Connector (resolves G0508). MuleSoft's X12 EDI Connector runs in a standard Mule app. It supports 005010X222A1 (837P) and 005010X221A1 (835) [docs: MuleSoft X12 EDI Connector 3.0, HIPAA versions]. Each payer's companion-guide rules are applied as overrides. - [assumption: connector schemas plus overrides cover the payers' guides; validate in trading-partner testing] - Anypoint Partner Manager is the fallback if managing the trading partners outgrows the app. - Delivery ID in the file. Each claim line ID goes into the 837 line item control number so the 835 can echo it back. - Safe resends. Sends are keyed on the batch ID, so a retry can't double-submit [KA-26029][KA-20501]. - Status updates. The batch status is written back to Salesforce, and a failed send raises a billing alert. - Credentials. Clearinghouse credentials live in MuleSoft secure properties. - Testing is at the partners' pace. PS helps create and adjust test files from Metz's sample 837s. Metz runs trading-partner testing with each clearinghouse. That testing happens on the outside parties' schedule and isn't compressed (G0001, G0002).

7. 835 Remittance Integration (E06)

Business context: - Billers download 835 files by hand from Availity and the Ohio.gov PASSPORT EDI portal, then work denials by status. - Metz keeps retrieving the files. - There are seven sources, one per clearinghouse.

Solution approach: - Metz drops the 835s into one SFTP folder. - MuleSoft polls the folder, parses the files and matches each payment line to the claim line through the echoed line ID. - The claim status and amount paid are updated. - Unmatched lines go to a review queue.

Supporting architecture: - Remittance history (resolves G0605, Option B). - A remittance header is created per 835 payment, plus one remittance line per claim line per 835. Each line holds the status, the amount paid, the adjustment reason codes and the payer claim control number. - The latest result rolls up to the claim line. - A delivery that's denied, rebilled, paid and later reversed keeps its full trail for the audit and for reconciling what was billed against what was paid. - [assumption: Metz needs this history; validate against audit lookback (G0901)] - Duplicate files. Re-reading the same 835 file is ignored, keyed on the file's control number and the payer [KA-26029]. - Scope boundary. E06 posts results and stops there. Cash posting to Certinia is Metz's later integration (G0616). - Test data. The build uses sample 835s. Live payer files confirm it in the first March billing run.

8. Audit Reporting and Record Locking (E09)

Business context: Metz is audited every year on deliveries made. The auditors want records that can't be changed after a certain point.

Solution approach: - Standard Salesforce reports cover deliveries, geotag and timestamps (from proof of delivery already in Metz's mobile app), authorizations and claims. - Deliveries, coverage periods and claims lock at defined points.

Supporting architecture: - How locking works. A status-driven record-type swap plus validation rules, with no custom code [KA-38980][KA-35088][KA-38982][KA-38963]. Billing supervisors get a controlled unlock permission for corrections. - Open. The lock points are open (G0012). - Change history. Field history is kept on coverage periods, claims and remittance lines [KA-11519]. - Pre-go-live history. Migrated records arrive with no change history. The old system's audit trail has to be archived outside Salesforce, as an export kept by Metz (G0902). - Consent and signatures are out of scope.

9. Data Migration and Person Account Conversion (E08)

Business context: Clients are Contacts in the live org today. About 13 months of delivery and claim history, plus active clients, funding sources, authorizations and open referrals, sit in an old system that ends on 31 March 2027.

Solution approach: 1. Convert the Contacts to person accounts in place. 2. Load the old system's data in dependency order. 3. Rehearse both steps in the full sandbox before production.

Supporting architecture: - In-place conversion (G0802 Option A). - Export, change to the Person Account record type and re-import with Data Loader. Record IDs are kept, so Cases, Funding Source, delivery and scheduling records and the mobile flows stay linked [KA-45684][KA-45683]. - Prerequisites: - one contact per account - the same owner and currency on both - relationship fields blank - no portal users [KA-4956][KA-37787] - Clients that fail the prerequisites are cleaned up first. 0003 — Each funding source is a Care Program; coverage periods are child records of a single enrollment

Date: 2026-11-06 · Status: accepted · Source: scopezilla-recommended

Context

Metz has about 40 funding sources (Medicaid waivers, managed care plans, county and city levies). A client can have only one active funding source at a time, and clients move between them ("Funding Source change"). Every delivery must tie back to the specific funding source in effect, and history is required for audit. The client already has a custom Funding Source object related to Contact. Health Cloud allows a given enrollee to be enrolled in a given care program only once, and blocks duplicate enrollments, including in bulk loads [KA-18914].

Decision

  • Each funding source is configured as a Health Cloud Care Program.
  • A client has one Care Program Enrollment per funding source, ever. A client who returns to a prior funding source has the existing enrollment reactivated rather than a new one created.
  • Each stretch of coverage (start date, end date, authorization reference, active flag) is a child coverage-period record under the enrollment. A validation rule allows only one active coverage period per client across all enrollments.
  • Deliveries and claims relate to the coverage period, which keeps the audit trail when a client moves (for example Anthem → CareSource → Anthem). Field history and record locking apply to coverage periods.

Consequences

  • The client's existing custom Funding Source object and its links to the delivery and scheduling objects must be mapped or migrated to this model. The scheduling objects themselves stay client-owned.
  • Migrated history must create one enrollment per client and funding source, with coverage periods underneath, to avoid the duplicate-enrollment error [KA-18914].
  • The 40 programs and their billing-rule attributes (meal quantity, delivery frequency, per-day vs. per-delivery billing) become Care Program configuration data.

Grounds

  • Scoping questionnaire p.1: "1. 40 / 2. Meal qty, delivery frequency / 3. No. We call it Funding Source change. You can only be in one active funding source at a time."
  • Scoping questionnaire p.2: "A related object to the contact called Funding Source… start date, end date, active checkbox. Validation rule that only one can be active at a time. Each delivery needs to tie back to that specific funding source… History is required for Audit purposes."
